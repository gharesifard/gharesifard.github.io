Seminar Overview

Mathematics and AI

This seminar explores the mathematics of AI and the connections between mathematics and artificial intelligence, including the mathematical foundations of AI and the questions that AI raises for mathematics. Topics may also include the mathematics behind AI methods and models, as well as AI tools for mathematical research. We plan to follow the talks with open discussions on research and education, providing an open forum for participants to share ideas, raise questions, and explore the role of AI in mathematical research, teaching, and learning.

  • August 10, 2026: Claude’s progress on the Riemann hypothesis — Anthropic announcement of an improved lower bound for the proportion of zeta zeros on the critical line. See also the shorter proof.
  • August 1, 2026: Ten advances in mathematics and theoretical computer science — OpenAI. Selected results announced on this date:
    • Sphere packing: Improved upper bounds for packing density in high dimensions, reaching the Cohn–Elkies threshold.
    • Non-sofic groups: A construction of groups that are not sofic.
    • Connes’s rigidity conjecture: A disproof concerning whether certain groups can be recovered uniquely from their von Neumann algebras.
    • Ehrhart’s volume conjecture: A sharp volume bound for convex bodies whose only interior lattice point is their centroid.
    • Multicolor Ramsey numbers: A lower bound growing faster than exponentially for triangle Ramsey numbers, addressing Erdős problem 183.
